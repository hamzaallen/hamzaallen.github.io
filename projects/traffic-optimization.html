Developing and evaluating intelligent intersection-control strategies in a simulated traffic environment.

01 / CASE STUDY

The problem

Traffic demand varies over time. A fixed sequence of signal phases may not respond to changing queues, directional imbalance or congestion. The engineering question is how different policies can be compared under common simulated conditions before considering real-world use.

Traffic signal optimization requires more than a single score: throughput, waiting time, queue buildup and phase behavior can move in different directions across scenarios.

02 / CASE STUDY

What I developed

Traffic simulation

Developed a simulation workflow using SUMO and the TraCI interface to inspect evolving vehicle and intersection states.

Controller comparison

Compared fixed-time traffic signals with Max Pressure and reinforcement-learning approaches using simulated scenarios.

Performance analysis

Examined controller behavior through traffic-related indicators such as delay, queues and throughput where collected.

Interactive visualization

Developed dashboard functionality for exploring simulation behavior and comparing control strategies.

03 / CASE STUDY

Three approaches to signal control

Fixed-time

A transparent reference policy following a preset schedule. Useful as a baseline for comparison.

Max Pressure

A feedback-oriented method that prioritizes phases using congestion or queue imbalance.

Reinforcement Learning

A learning-based control approach assessed against non-learning policies in simulation.

The purpose of the comparison is to explore control-policy behavior and tradeoffs, not to presume that any approach is universally superior.

05 / CASE STUDY

Evaluation focus

The comparative approach considers waiting time, queue behavior, network throughput and signal phase dynamics. Interpretation depends on scenario design, random seeds and comparable evaluation conditions.

Evidence boundary

This public case study does not present numerical improvements or claim a proven best controller; reproducible experiment outputs and independent benchmarking would be required to support quantitative claims.

06 / CASE STUDY

Limitations and next steps

Simulation results depend on traffic demand assumptions and network configuration. A stronger engineering validation would document held-out scenarios, repeated runs, controller training and evaluation separation, runtime constraints and safety rules for phase changes.

The framework has not been presented here as a production-controlled physical intersection or a real-world traffic deployment.

Engineering lesson

Adaptive decision-making should be tested against simple, credible baselines under matched conditions. Reliable comparisons matter more than optimistic benchmark claims.
