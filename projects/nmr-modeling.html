THE PROJECT IN 20 SECONDS

Built a spectroscopy-to-prediction workflow to estimate crude-oil properties from NMR data. The key engineering challenge was reliable preprocessing and evaluation on a small dataset with repeated measurements.

01 / CASE STUDY

The problem

Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) signals encode information about a sample's chemical composition. Predicting useful properties such as API gravity from these signals can reduce reliance on manual interpretation, but raw measurements are noisy and repeated scans are not independent samples.

The challenge is to construct reproducible features and estimate performance on unseen physical samples, not just additional scans of known samples.

02 / CASE STUDY

What I implemented

Signal processing

Developed a pipeline from free-induction decay to frequency-domain spectra, including FFT, phase/baseline correction and reference alignment.

Feature preparation

Explored normalization, ppm selection and binning as versioned preprocessing decisions.

Statistical modeling

Evaluated partial least squares (PLS) and principal component regression (PCR), including target transformations.

Leakage-aware validation

Grouped replicates by source sample so correlated scans did not appear on both sides of a cross-validation split.

05 / CASE STUDY

What stronger evidence would require

New batches, new instruments or changed acquisition protocols may shift the spectral distribution. Prospective external evaluation, artifact versioning, drift checks and a packaged preprocessing-to-prediction pipeline are the next steps.

Engineering lesson

Reliable machine learning starts with the validation design. A high R² matters only when the split reflects the task the model will face.
